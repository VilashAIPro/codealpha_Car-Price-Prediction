About the Project

What this project solves

An end-to-end ML pipeline using Python, Pandas, Scikit-learn, and Matplotlib to predict second-hand car prices in the Indian automobile market.

📊

Brand & Feature Collection

Collected real-world car listings from CarDekho — brand goodwill via present price, year, fuel type, transmission, mileage, and more across 301 vehicles.

🔧

Feature Engineering

Engineered Car_Age from year, computed depreciation %, label-encoded categorical variables, and scaled with StandardScaler.

🤖

Model Training

Trained three regression models with 80/20 split — Linear Regression, Random Forest (100 trees), and Gradient Boosting (100 estimators), all with random_state=42.

📈

Model Evaluation

Evaluated each model on R², MAE and RMSE. Gradient Boosting achieved R²=0.9641, meaning 96.4% of price variance is explained by the 7 input features.

🎨

8 Visualisations

Generated 8 publication-quality dark-themed charts using Matplotlib and Seaborn — distributions, heatmaps, scatter plots, depreciation curve, and feature importance.

🌍

Real-World Impact

Helps buyers estimate fair used car prices and helps sellers set competitive prices — reducing information asymmetry in the ₹4+ Lakh average Indian used car market.

Key Takeaways

Project Insights

The most significant patterns discovered during EDA and model analysis.

🔑

Top Predictor

Present_Price is the strongest predictor with 0.88 correlation to Selling_Price. Current showroom price drives resale value more than any other feature.

⛽

Diesel Premium

Diesel cars command a massive +₹7.02 Lakh premium over petrol (avg ₹10.28L vs ₹3.26L) due to better fuel efficiency and torque for long-distance use.

⚙️

Automatic Luxury Tax

Automatic transmission cars sell at +₹5.49 Lakh more than manuals (avg ₹9.42L vs ₹3.93L) — reflecting their premium positioning in the Indian market.

📉

Depreciation Curve

Cars depreciate by an average of 36.6% from showroom price. The steepest drop occurs within the first 3–5 years — confirming the Car_Age feature's value.

🚀

Gradient Boosting Wins

Gradient Boosting outperforms all models with R²=0.9641, predicting prices within ±₹0.56 Lakhs on average — 2× better than linear regression's ±₹1.22L.

👤

First-Owner Dominance

First-owner cars (prev_owners=0) represent 96.3% of the dataset (290 records). Multiple-owner cars show measurable extra depreciation beyond fuel and transmission effects.
